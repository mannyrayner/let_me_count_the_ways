# Expressive/reflexive E: development note

Review of the v0.3 *Madame Bovary* annotations raised a possible conflation
between two phenomena:

1. emotionally intense delivery of a truth-conditional or performative love
   utterance; and
2. genuinely expressive/reflexive production of the words.

The contrast matters because an utterance can be shouted, spontaneous,
passionate, repeated, or sexually charged while still primarily putting forward
the proposition that the speaker loves the addressee. Delivery alone does not
make the act expressive/reflexive.

Discussion compared “Ouch!” with an angry use of “Asshole!”:

```text
Driver: “Asshole!”
Passenger: “Actually, I know him. He’s a nice guy.”
Driver: “Maybe. I was just furious.”
```

The passenger can challenge a proposition naturally associated with the word,
even though the driver's original act may primarily have been an expressive
outburst. Expressive material can therefore license propositional
interpretation and contradiction without the original act necessarily being an
assertion. Simple diagnostics based only on whether negation or contradiction
is possible are consequently weak.

A more useful provisional question is:

> Was the proposition being put forward for acceptance as true, or did the
> linguistic material primarily function as expressive discharge?

This does not settle whether E is a core semantic/pragmatic type, an orthogonal
expressive dimension, reducible to T/P plus affect, or a stable mixed force.
Open research questions include whether clear literary cases occur, whether E
can coexist with truth-conditional content, and whether love utterances pattern
like expressive insults or interjections. Prompt v0.3.1 operationalises a
sharper current hypothesis without deciding those theoretical questions.
