\subsection{Thresholds and status}

At support threshold 2, the combined material has 189 T-only, 41 T-and-P, 17 T-and-E, one T/P/E and four records with none of T/P/E reaching the threshold. At threshold 3 the counts are 217 T-only, 21 T-and-P, seven T-and-E, two P-only and five with none. The two P-only records at threshold 3 both have T2/P4: Boarham's conditional offer and Emma's promise-like inducement. No occurrence has P at least 3 with T at most 1.

These descriptions concern thresholds on ordinal judgments, not latent classes. The 195 direct-speech records have their own table column; hypothetical, quoted, written and nonverbal-verbalized material is not silently treated as direct conversation. All 27 extension records have T4. A hypothesis about the absence of affection requires an additional variable, rather than interpreting T as a sincerity score.

\subsection{Questions for the next revision}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Unit of force.} Specify whether P concerns the love clause, the surrounding speech turn or the scene. Compare Henny's explicit acceptance, Wentworth's letter and Lucy's unspoken resolutions.
\item \textbf{Affection and undertaking.} Distinguish the represented presence of affection from an avowal's truth-presenting force. Separate intended commitment, proposed terms, acceptance and coercive pressure.
\item \textbf{E and narrative evidence.} Decide how metaphors of overflow relate to explicit failures of verbal inhibition. Keep words ascribed to a look distinct from words actually spoken.
\item \textbf{Context sensitivity.} Examine whether local windows omit information needed for selected judgments, and compare controlled increases in context. Corpus availability is not evidence that the annotator used the whole work.
\item \textbf{Independent assessment.} Obtain independent readings or a designed comparison of prompts and contexts before treating numerical support scores as validated measurements.
\item \textbf{Literature and genre.} Extend engagement with speech-act and literary scholarship. A claim about genre differences requires an explicitly selected comparison corpus; one licensed romance and a targeted historical extension are insufficient.
\end{enumerate}

The five-work extension was selected after discussion of duty and economic dependence. That history is part of the evidence, not a preregistration. Similarly, new extraction patterns were developed during corpus work. The distinction between exploratory development and any later held-out test should remain explicit.

\subsection{Quotation checks and external illustrations}

Quoted scored passages were checked against their saved requests and canonical sources. The quoted Skram passages were also checked against first-edition scans: \emph{Constance Ring}, pp.~137, 309 and 370; \emph{Lucie}, p.~176. The repository source maps link OCR offsets to page images. These checks do not amount to proofreading either complete OCR text. Spellings follow the chosen edition, except that the corpus \emph{Et dukkehjem} is a documented modernization. Translations of quoted non-English passages in the article are the authors' analytical renderings.

The rose, Dug and the \emph{Ghost} dialogue are introductory illustrations outside the scored dataset. The movie references cite screenplays, not timed transcriptions of released films. The Tolstoy and Lear comparisons are also unscored. The \emph{Pengar} wedding-formula passage and Monica's evasion are qualitative evidence outside the retained explicit-ILY set. \emph{Splash} is cited as the earlier essay's proposed idealized convergence; no fresh scene annotation is claimed.
