## Original AI explanation

Cyrano’s “Je t’aime” is unequivocally a sincere avowal of his loving state (T). It also has independently supported expressive force: he explicitly abandons polished artifice and describes himself as flinging out whatever words come to him, with the utterance emerging amid emotional overwhelm (E). It does not itself undertake a relational commitment, and T plus E adequately represent its core force.

## Ambiguity

The degree of E is somewhat interpretive because this remains an eloquent dramatic declaration; however, the explicit framing as an unarranged outpouring from the heart supports more than merely passionate delivery. Roxane’s mistaken attribution of the speech to Christian complicates its reception, not its core force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

The declaration is sincere as Cyrano’s avowal but occurs within an identity deception: Roxane understands the voice and words as Christian’s. Cyrano stresses that he is finally speaking from his “true heart,” without the wit and artifice that previously screened his feelings. His promise to throw out the words as they come, “without arranging them in a bouquet,” and the surrounding language of suffocation and emotional excess present the words partly as affective overflow. The subsequent elaboration confirms enduring, attentive, self-sacrificing love, while Roxane explicitly recognizes the speech as love. None of this makes the highlighted words a relational undertaking by Cyrano, since he remains hidden and cannot openly establish such a commitment with her.
