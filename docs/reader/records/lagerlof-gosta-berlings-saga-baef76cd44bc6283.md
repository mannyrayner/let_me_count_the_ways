## Original AI explanation

Marianne’s utterance is principally an avowal that Gösta has always been the exclusive object of her love. Its immediate strategic and possibly self-deceptive use does not diminish its truth-conditional mode. It does not itself undertake a commitment, and the narration portrays it as a deliberate attempt to perform passion rather than an affect-driven verbal discharge.

## Ambiguity

The sincerity and self-knowledge behind the avowal are unstable: Marianne is consciously trying to win Gösta back and cannot then access a wholehearted feeling, yet the subsequent narration says her love had not been dead and becomes fully conscious only after he leaves. This affects interpretation of truthfulness, not the utterance’s dominant T force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Marianne says this while deliberately attempting to regain Gösta and to appear passionate; Gösta explicitly rejects it as a lie. The narrator initially emphasizes her cold self-observation and theatrical effort, but after Gösta leaves depicts her love as real though previously suppressed or inaccessible during her weakened condition. The utterance is therefore a strategically deployed and imperfectly self-aware avowal whose underlying content receives retrospective confirmation. Its urgency and surrounding pleas do not independently make it performative or expressive/reflexive.
