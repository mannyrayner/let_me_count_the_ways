## Original AI explanation

Gerald’s why-question presupposes or provisionally concedes that he does not love Gudrun and asks for the reason. Its core force is therefore truth-oriented: it presents the absence of a loving state as true or admitted. It neither undertakes a relational commitment nor functions as a reflex-like affective discharge.

## Ambiguity

The interrogative form literally requests an explanation, but the narrator explicitly frames it as an admission, strongly supporting T rather than a merely information-seeking reading.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Gudrun has coldly claimed that Gerald loves her “very little indeed.” His response effectively accepts her accusation while expressing hostility toward her for compelling that admission. The subsequent exchange confirms this interpretation: he repeatedly denies having loved her or being able to love her. His anger and emotional distress characterize the delivery and conflict but do not independently establish expressive/reflexive force.
