## Original AI explanation

The utterance primarily presents Adolphe's love as presently true, so it has strong truth-conditional force even though the narration strongly suggests that the avowal is false or self-deceptive. In immediately reversing an attempted separation and asking Ellénore to return to him, it also has moderate force as a renewal of their relationship, though it contains no explicit promise. His crying out in an emotional crisis indicates intense delivery but does not independently establish expressive/reflexive force.

## Ambiguity

The extent of performative force is uncertain: the avowal effectively renews the relationship, but it may principally be an expedient reassurance rather than a deliberate undertaking. Adolphe may retain affection despite lacking the passionate love he claims.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Adolphe has just told Ellénore that he no longer feels passionate love, intending to free himself and induce her to accept the count's offer. After she collapses, he reverses himself and claims both that he loves her tenderly and that his prior denial was a benevolent deception. Ellénore believes the avowal and renews her sacrifice, while the narrator undercuts it by saying that she mistakes her own love for a mutual one. Thus the utterance operates as a likely deceptive or self-deceptive avowal and relational reassurance whose practical effect is to bind Adolphe more deeply.
