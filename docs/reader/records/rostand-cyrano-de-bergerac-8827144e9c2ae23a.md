The embedded “Je t’aime!” represents a straightforward avowal of loving feeling. Its imagined power to transform the ashamed prince is a perlocutionary fairy-tale effect, not a relational undertaking, and there is no independent evidence that the words function as reflex-like affective discharge.

## Ambiguity

The phrase is simultaneously a generic fairy-tale quotation and Cyrano’s revoicing of Roxane’s immediately preceding declaration, but either construal gives it predominantly truth-conditional force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

After Roxane declares “Je vous aime, vivez!”, Cyrano revoices her declaration as the fairy-tale words spoken to a shame-filled, ugly prince. He contrasts their miraculous effect in stories with his own unchanged appearance. This self-deprecating framing and the words’ hoped-for emotional impact do not change the represented love formula from an avowal into a commitment or expressive discharge.
