## Original AI explanation

Emma’s repeated “Comme je t’aime” directly avows intense maternal love for Berthe, giving T maximal support. It does not undertake or renew a relational obligation, so P is unsupported. The sudden movement from nostalgic distress to rushing at Berthe, repeated exclamative wording, and the narration’s characterization of the episode as an “excès de tendresse” provide some independent evidence that the words are an affective outpouring rather than only an emphatic assertion, supporting moderate E.

## Ambiguity

E is the main uncertainty: the scene presents an impulsive emotional effusion, but its expressive force could also be represented as a very intense, spontaneous T-avowal.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

After reading her father’s affectionate letter and mourning her lost happiness and illusions, Emma hears Berthe laughing, rushes to embrace her, and twice declares her love. The tenderness appears genuine in the moment but unusually sudden and excessive: Emma cleans and fusses over Berthe, kisses her, weeps, and then returns her to the servant, who is astonished. This framing suggests a transient surge of maternal feeling shaped by Emma’s nostalgia and unhappiness, without converting the declaration into a commitment or undermining its avowal force.
