## Original AI explanation

“I will love you always” presents Gerald’s future and enduring love as true while, more strongly, taking the conventional form of a promise or relational undertaking. His coercion, agony, and probable insincerity do not remove those forces. The words are forced out rather than produced as a reflex-like affective discharge, so E lacks independent support.

## Ambiguity

The future construction supports both a prediction/avowal of continuing love (T) and a promise-like undertaking (P); the immediate demand to say he will love her forever makes P dominant.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Gudrun demands a declaration even if it is false, after Gerald has repeatedly denied loving her or ever doing so. He complies in agony, apparently as coerced verbal submission rather than sincere reassurance, and she immediately treats his saying it with raillery. This undermines sincerity and voluntariness but is compatible with the utterance’s truth-presenting and promise-like force.
