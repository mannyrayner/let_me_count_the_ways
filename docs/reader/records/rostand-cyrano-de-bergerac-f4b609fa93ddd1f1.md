## Original passage

> ROXANE:
>   Je t'aimerais encore !
>   Si toute ta beauté tout d'un coup s'envolait. . .


## Original AI explanation

Roxane’s conditional “Je t’aimerais encore” directly avows that her love would persist even if Christian lost his beauty. It presents a counterfactual loving disposition as true, rather than undertaking a new relational commitment. The exclamation and emotional setting intensify the avowal but provide no independent evidence of expressive/reflex-like discharge.

## Ambiguity

The future-oriented conditional can sound pledge-like, especially when Roxane later says “je le jure,” but its core force is a claim about the persistence and basis of her existing love, not a relational undertaking.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Roxane is correcting her earlier, beauty-based attraction and insisting that she now loves what she takes to be Christian’s soul. Her statement answers Christian’s doubt and reassures him by asserting that ugliness would not alter her love. Dramatic irony complicates the addressee and object of that love: the soul disclosed through the voice and letters is substantially Cyrano’s, and Christian immediately concludes that she therefore loves Cyrano. This misidentification affects the utterance’s truth and narrative import, but not its predominantly truth-conditional force.
