## Original AI explanation

Roxane directly avows a past stage of her love: she loved the addressee for his beauty and soul together. The utterance presents an emotional state as true and helps narrate its development, but it does not itself undertake a commitment. Its poetic and impassioned delivery supplies no independent evidence of expressive/reflex-like discharge.

## Ambiguity

Roxane addresses Christian, but the surrounding dramatic irony divides the qualities she loves between Christian's beauty and Cyrano's words or perceived soul. This complicates the effective object of love without changing the utterance's strongly truth-conditional force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

The line forms the middle step in Roxane's account of her changing love: first Christian's beauty alone, then beauty and soul together, and now the soul alone. She sincerely presents this progression to Christian, unaware that the letters and eloquent language through which she apprehends his soul came from Cyrano. The resulting dramatic irony concerns mistaken attribution, not the core utterance's mode.
