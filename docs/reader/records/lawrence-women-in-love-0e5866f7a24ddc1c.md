## Original AI explanation

The speaker directly and emphatically avows love as a settled, enduring emotional fact. “It’s final” gives the avowal limited commitment-like force, but he primarily presents his love as something already certain rather than undertaking a new obligation. His controlled, almost impatient response supplies no independent evidence of expressive/reflexive discharge.

## Ambiguity

“Final” may imply lasting relational commitment, but the phrasing is more explicitly constative—“I know”—than promissory or undertaking.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

The avowal occurs after conflict over what love demands and after the woman repeatedly presses for reassurance. His “Yes, I do” answers her question, while “I know it’s final” presents his love as certain and settled. His wish to stop further questioning makes the delivery restrained and impatient rather than reflex-like. Her subsequent “delight and doubt” shows that she receives the declaration as meaningful reassurance but remains uncertain about it.
