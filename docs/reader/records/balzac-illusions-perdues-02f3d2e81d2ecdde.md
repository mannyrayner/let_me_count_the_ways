## Original AI explanation

Coralie’s “Je t’aimerai mieux en ne te trompant point” primarily presents as true a future loving attitude toward Camusot: she will love him better once she is no longer deceiving him. In conjunction with her invitation to continue visiting, the future tense also gives the avowal a moderate promise-like or relationally undertaking force. Nothing indicates that the words are a reflexive affective discharge.

## Ambiguity

The future form can function both as a prediction of future feeling and as an assurance or undertaking. “Mieux” may mean loving him better in quality rather than loving him more intensely.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Coralie has openly chosen Lucien while allowing Camusot to keep visiting and retaining the material arrangement he supports. Her statement offers Camusot a more honest, qualified form of affection rather than exclusive romantic fidelity. Its potentially consoling or instrumental role does not alter its predominantly truth-conditional and moderately undertaking force.
