## Original passage

> CYRANO:
>   Non, non, mon cher amour, je ne vous aimais pas !


## Original AI explanation

Cyrano’s direct assertion literally denies having loved Roxane, but its transparent falsity in context makes it pragmatically function as an indirect avowal of the very love he denies. Its core force remains truth-conditional: it presents a proposition about his loving state, without undertaking a relational commitment. Repetition, weakening delivery, and emotional struggle intensify the denial but do not independently establish expressive/reflexive force.

## Ambiguity

There is a sharp divergence between the literal denial and the contextually understood avowal, but both are truth-conditional uses rather than an ontology failure.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Roxane has recognized that Cyrano authored the letters and supplied the balcony voice, exposing his fourteen-year concealment. Cyrano continues the self-effacing imposture by denying his love, but the endearment “mon cher amour,” his weakening voice, Roxane’s confident contradiction, and the accumulated evidence make the denial transparently unconvincing. The line therefore operates dramatically as a self-cancelling denial and indirect confession, not as a promise or undertaking.
