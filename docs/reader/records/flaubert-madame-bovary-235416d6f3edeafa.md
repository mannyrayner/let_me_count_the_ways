## Original AI explanation

Charles directly presents his continuing love for Emma as an established fact: “Tu sais bien que je t’aime.” Its immediate purpose is to reassure and persuade her to calm down and approach him, but it does not itself undertake a new relational commitment. Although prompted by tenderness, discouragement, and distress, the wording is a deliberate avowal rather than an expressive or reflex-like discharge.

## Ambiguity

The utterance invokes their existing marital relationship and has a conciliatory aim, but reassurance and relational appeal alone do not supply independent performative force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Charles cannot comprehend Emma’s intense hostility and responds by reminding her of what he treats as mutually known—his love—while urging her to calm herself and come to him. The avowal is sincere within the presented scene but tragically ineffective: Emma is inwardly estranged from him, preoccupied with Rodolphe, and answers “Assez!” before fleeing. This dramatic irony affects reception and narrative meaning, not the core utterance’s predominantly truth-conditional force.
