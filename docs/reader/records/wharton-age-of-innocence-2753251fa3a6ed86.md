## Original AI explanation

Archer’s “At least I loved you” is principally a retrospective avowal presented as true: whatever their misunderstandings and losses, his love for Ellen was real. It does not itself undertake a future commitment. His difficulty in bringing out the words conveys emotional strain, but there is no independent evidence that they function as a reflex-like expressive discharge rather than a deeply felt assertion.

## Ambiguity

The past tense may frame the love as part of a seemingly lost possibility rather than imply that it has ended; the subsequent embrace indicates continuing feeling. The strained delivery could weakly suggest expressive force, but not enough to warrant E support under the narrow criterion.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Archer has just discovered that his own advice helped make a relationship with Ellen impossible. Under the perceived weight of something “final and irrevocable,” he offers the avowal as a minimal vindication or consolation: at least his love was genuine. Ellen’s crying, their embrace, and his immediate insistence that matters can still be undone reveal mutual and continuing affect, but the classified sentence itself remains an avowal rather than a commitment. Its ellipsis and “he brought out” mark painful delivery without establishing independent expressive/reflexive force.
