There is an elegy by Propertius that portrays, better than any other work of antiquity, this dignity of women among the Romans, more imposing and purer than even the splendor they enjoyed during the age of chivalry. Cornelia, who died in her youth, addresses the most touching farewells and consolations to her husband, and in it one feels, in almost every word, all that is worthy of respect and sacred in family bonds.
