Its curt delivery supplies no positive evidence that the words are an affect-driven expressive or reflex-like discharge.

## Ambiguity

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

The avowal occurs during a tense retrospective dispute about whether Everard had been serious and whether either party truly loved the other. It functions as his concise defense against Rhoda’s accusation. His subsequent “I loved you, that’s all” reinforces the avowal, while “I believe I still love you” and his renewed marriage proposal are distinct utterances. Rhoda later challenges the entire sincerity of his love, creating uncertainty about truthfulness but not about the core utterance’s assertive force.
