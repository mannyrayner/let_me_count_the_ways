## Original AI explanation

Everard’s “I believe I still love you” is principally a hedged present-state avowal: he presents his continuing love as true while marking some uncertainty about it. The utterance does not itself undertake a commitment, despite occurring immediately before a marriage proposal, and its controlled argumentative delivery provides no independent expressive/reflexive force.

## Ambiguity

The epistemic hedge “I believe” weakens Everard’s certainty and may suggest imperfect self-knowledge, but it does not alter the utterance’s predominantly truth-conditional mode.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

The avowal answers Rhoda’s challenge to Everard’s seriousness during a tense reconsideration of their former relationship. Its proximity to repeated marriage proposals gives it courtship and persuasive significance, but the proposal—not the love avowal itself—carries the clear undertaking force. Rhoda subsequently disputes the sincerity and completeness of his love, and Everard soon resolves to marry Agnes Brissenden, creating contextual doubt about the depth or durability of the state he avows without changing the utterance’s truth-conditional force.
