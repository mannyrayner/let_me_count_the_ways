## Original passage

## Original AI explanation

Armand presents his love for Marguerite as an established fact, reminding her of it to counter her fear that his father may persuade him to abandon her. The utterance is therefore strongly truth-conditional. Its reassuring function and the surrounding intention to remain together do not make the words themselves a relational undertaking, and there is no positive evidence of expressive or reflex-like discharge.

## Ambiguity

The reassurance pragmatically suggests steadfastness, but it does not itself explicitly undertake or renew a commitment, so this does not warrant substantial P support.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Marguerite fears that Armand's father will use her past to persuade Armand to leave her. Armand answers by invoking her existing knowledge of his love, chiefly as reassurance and as a reason she should discount that possibility. His surrounding refusal to abandon her and his plan to persuade his father strengthen the sincerity and constancy attributed to the avowal, while the later plot pressure does not alter its core force here.
