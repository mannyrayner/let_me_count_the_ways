## Original AI explanation

Christian's direct “Je t'aime” is principally an avowal presenting his love for Roxane as true. Nothing in the utterance independently undertakes a commitment, and the deliberate courtship exchange provides no evidence that the words are a reflex-like affective discharge rather than an emotionally invested assertion.

## Ambiguity

Christian's desire and limited eloquence may complicate the depth or character of his feelings, but not the utterance's strongly truth-conditional mode.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Christian has rejected Cyrano's help and deliberately attempts to speak for himself. Roxane accepts that he loves her but demands an eloquent account of how he loves her; thus the comic failure concerns his inability to elaborate, not the semantic force of this avowal. The exclamation mark and romantic intensity do not independently establish expressive/reflexive force, while the courtship setting alone does not make the utterance a relational undertaking.
