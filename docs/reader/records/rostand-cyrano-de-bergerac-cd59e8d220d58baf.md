## Original passage

## Original AI explanation

Roxane directly avows that she loves the person she believes she is addressing. The coordinated declaration “et suis tienne” (“and I am yours”) also gives the exchange substantial relational-undertaking force. Her trembling, tears, and intoxication establish intense affective delivery but do not independently show that “je t’aime” is a reflex-like verbal discharge rather than a passionate avowal and undertaking.

## Ambiguity

The performative force depends partly on whether “I am yours” is heard as an enacted commitment or as a figurative description of emotional surrender; either reading leaves T dominant.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Roxane responds to Cyrano’s impassioned declaration while believing its words and voice belong to Christian. Her love avowal is sincere as presented, but its intended object is bound up with mistaken authorship: she attributes Cyrano’s eloquence and emotional disclosure to Christian. The deception and dramatic irony complicate whom she understands herself to love, without changing the utterance’s core avowal and relational-undertaking force.
