## Original AI explanation

Lorck’s “Jeg elskede dig, Conny” (“I loved you, Conny”) is a direct retrospective avowal presenting his past love as true. It does not independently undertake or renew a commitment, and nothing indicates that the words function as a reflex-like affective discharge rather than an affectionate assertion.

## Ambiguity

The intimate marital setting gives the avowal a reassuring relational effect, but that effect does not by itself establish performative force. The past tense may invite an inference about continuing love, though the immediate emphasis is on the history of his feelings.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Lorck says this while the couple recall his courtship and Constance’s former reluctance. His subsequent claim that he had never loved before loving her reinforces the utterance as a retrospective avowal of genuine, uniquely significant love. Although the scene is physically tender and the statement helps reassure Constance, neither tenderness nor reassurance supplies independent expressive/reflexive or commitment-making force.
