Adolfo’s retrospective “ti ho amata davvero, onestamente” (“I truly, honestly loved you”) explicitly presents his past love for Loredana as true. It makes no present relational undertaking, and its force is controlled avowal used within an accusation rather than expressive or reflex-like discharge.

The narration frames the speech as deliberately cruel: he is driven to trample and destroy his love and takes satisfaction in her defenselessness, before remorse follows. This weaponized and possibly self-conflicted use affects the interpersonal meaning but does not alter the core utterance’s dominant truth-conditional force.
