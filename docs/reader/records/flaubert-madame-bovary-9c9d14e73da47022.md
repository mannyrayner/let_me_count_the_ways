## Original AI explanation

Emma’s “je t’aime” primarily avows her consuming love for Rodolphe. It also has independently supported expressive force: the narration interprets such language as the possible overflowing of a full soul, making the words an affective discharge rather than merely an impassioned assertion. It does not itself clearly undertake a commitment or obligation.

## Ambiguity

The degree of E is somewhat uncertain because this is recurrent, conventionalized lover’s language rather than an explicitly involuntary blurt; nevertheless, the narrator’s overflow metaphor supplies unusually direct positive evidence for expressive force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Emma speaks amid jealousy, dependency, tears, and demands for reassurance. Rodolphe discounts her declarations because he has heard similar clichés from other lovers, but the narrator explicitly distinguishes sameness of expression from difference of feeling and suggests that Emma’s exaggerated, conventional metaphors can be the overflow of genuine inner plenitude. Her sincerity and emotional excess support T and E; her surrounding declarations of submission do not turn the highlighted love avowal itself into a clear relational undertaking.
