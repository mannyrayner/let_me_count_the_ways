## Original passage

## Original AI explanation

Cyrano directly presents as true the negative proposition that he did not love Roxane. Although the denial is transparently insincere in context, it remains fully truth-conditional in force: T classifies the avowal or report, not its truthfulness. The line neither undertakes a relational commitment nor independently functions as an affect-driven verbal discharge.

## Ambiguity

The dramatic context reverses the denial's credibility, but not its core assertive force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

The denial is a deliberate, self-effacing falsehood meant to preserve Cyrano's long-standing sacrifice and continue assigning the love to Christian. Roxane immediately rejects it, and Cyrano's subsequent weakening denials—including the revealing address “mon cher amour”—make his love unmistakable. This deception affects sincerity and dramatic meaning but does not require a category beyond T/P/E.
