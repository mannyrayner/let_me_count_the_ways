## Original AI explanation

The repeated words are principally a sincere avowal of Birkin’s love: the narrator explicitly says he speaks “with truth.” Their marital setting gives them a faint undert undertaking resonance, but the phrase does not independently institute or promise the relationship. Repetition, whispering, trembling, and intense gladness do not establish expressive/reflexive force. The narrator’s claim that the formula fails to capture a transcendent oneness qualifies the avowal’s adequacy without changing its core force into an ontological remainder.

## Ambiguity

The surrounding marriage and language of union weakly support P. The narrator’s metaphysical rejection of “I” and “you” creates some pressure toward O, but it concerns the conventional proposition’s inability to describe the full experience, not a distinct core speech-act force.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Birkin sincerely loves Ursula, but the narrator presents ordinary love-language as radically inadequate to what the moment means to him: rebirth, gratitude, self-transcendence, and a union in which separate “I” and “you” disappear. Ursula cannot perceive this significance and instead wants more explicit proof and adoration. Thus the utterance is truthfully meant yet fails, in the narrative’s metaphysical framing, to communicate Birkin’s whole experience.
