## Original passage

> KENT.
> I must love you, and sue to know you better.


## Original AI explanation

Kent’s modal “I must love you” is primarily a courteous prospective undertaking to regard Edmund favorably and cultivate acquaintance. It weakly presents an affiliative disposition as true, but does not straightforwardly avow an already established loving emotion. Nothing indicates that the words are a reflex-like affective discharge.

## Ambiguity

“Must” can express both felt inevitability and social obligation; the immediate first-meeting context favors a conventional affiliative undertaking over a report of deep present feeling.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

Gloucester introduces Kent as an honorable friend and asks Edmund to remember him; Kent responds by undertaking to favor Edmund and seek closer acquaintance. Edmund’s promise to “study deserving” confirms that the exchange establishes prospective mutual regard within a formal courtly introduction. The utterance is courteous and potentially sincere, but not an impassioned declaration.
