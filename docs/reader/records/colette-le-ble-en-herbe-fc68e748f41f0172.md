## Original AI explanation

Within the narrator’s imagined future scene, “Je t’aime” primarily avows Vinca’s love for Phil. Its placement among words of surrender gives it slight commitment-like force, but the explicit undertaking is chiefly carried by the adjacent “fais de moi ce que tu voudras,” not by the love avowal itself. Nothing indicates that the words function as a reflex-like affective discharge.

## Ambiguity

Because the utterance occurs inside a fantasy of Vinca’s future capitulation, it has some relational-undertaking coloration; however, this does not warrant strong P support for the core phrase.

## Narrative situation (AI interpretation)

The narrator imagines that Vinca may next year fall at Phil’s feet and speak “a woman’s words,” including “I love you,” pleas for attention, and permission for him to do as he wishes. This frames the avowal as part of anticipated emotional and relational surrender. The contrast with her present childish dignity and resistance also suggests that the imagined words represent a future transition into adult-coded submission. Phil’s dislike of her resistance and desire that she be exclusively promised and available add an asymmetrical, possessive context, but they do not alter the core phrase’s predominantly truth-conditional force.
