How the scores were assigned

Cyrano’s direct assertion literally denies having loved Roxane, but its transparent falsity in context makes it pragmatically function as an indirect avowal of the very love he denies. Its core force remains truth-conditional: it presents a proposition about his loving state, without undertaking a relational commitment. Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Roxane has recognized that Cyrano authored the letters and supplied the balcony voice, exposing his fourteen-year concealment. Cyrano continues the self-effacing imposture by denying his love, but the endearment “mon cher amour,” his weakening voice, Roxane’s confident contradiction, and the accumulated evidence make the denial transparently unconvincing. The line therefore operates dramatically as a self-cancelling denial and indirect confession, not as a promise or undertaking.

CYRANO (reading): It is to be this evening, I think, my beloved! My soul is still heavy with unexpressed love, And I am dying! Never again, never will my intoxicated eyes, My gaze, whose. . . ROXANE: How you read it, His letter! CYRANO (continuing): . . .whose thrilling delight it was, Kiss in passing the gestures that you make; I see again a little one familiar to you, With which you touch your brow, and I should like to cry out. . . ROXANE (disturbed): How you read it—this letter! (Night falls imperceptibly.) CYRANO: And I cry: Farewell! . . . ROXANE: You read it. . . CYRANO: My dear one, my darling, My treasure. . . ROXANE (dreamily): In a voice. . . CYRANO: My love! . . . ROXANE: In a voice. . . (She starts): But. . . which I am not hearing for the first time! (She approaches very softly, without his noticing, passes behind the armchair, bends over noiselessly, and looks at the letter.—The shadows deepen.) CYRANO: My heart never left you for a single second, And I am and shall be, even in the other world, He who loved you beyond measure, he who. . . ROXANE (placing her hand on his shoulder): How can you read now? It is dark. (He starts, turns, sees her there very close to him, makes a frightened gesture, and bows his head. A long silence. Then, in the now complete darkness, she speaks slowly, clasping her hands): And for fourteen years, he played this part: Being the old friend who comes to be amusing! CYRANO: Roxane! ROXANE: It was you! CYRANO: No, no, Roxane, no! ROXANE: I should have guessed when he spoke my name! CYRANO: No, it was not I! ROXANE: It was you! CYRANO: I swear to you. . . ROXANE: I see the whole generous imposture: The letters—it was you. . . CYRANO: No! ROXANE: The tender, wild words, It was you. . . CYRANO: No! ROXANE: The voice in the night—it was you! CYRANO: I swear to you it was not! ROXANE: The soul—it was yours! CYRANO: I did not love you. ROXANE: You loved me! CYRANO (struggling): It was the other man! ROXANE: You loved me!
