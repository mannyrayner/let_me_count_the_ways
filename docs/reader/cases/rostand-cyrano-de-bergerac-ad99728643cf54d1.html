How the scores were assigned

Christian's negative sentence is a rhetorical scalar correction: in the sequence “Je t'aime !” / “je ne t'aime pas !” / “Je t'adore !”, it rejects “love” only to replace it with the purportedly stronger “adore.” Its pragmatic force is therefore a truth-conditional avowal of exceptionally strong love, not a genuine retraction. It undertakes no relational commitment, and the quick, emphatic delivery does not independently establish expressive/reflexive force.

Ambiguity noted by the annotator

In isolation the sentence literally denies love, but the immediately following “Je t'adore !” strongly resolves it as an intensified avowal rather than a sincere denial.

Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Roxane has grown impatient with Christian's repetitive, ineloquent declarations. As she starts to leave, he hurriedly varies the formula by denying the weaker term and substituting “I adore you,” hoping intensification will retain her interest. Roxane understands that he loves her but rejects his lack of eloquence. The line is comically unsuccessful rhetoric, not evidence that Christian lacks loving or desirous feelings.
