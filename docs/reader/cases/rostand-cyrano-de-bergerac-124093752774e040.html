Ambiguity noted by the annotator

The surrounding declaration conveys profound devotion, but the highlighted clause primarily describes and clarifies Roxane's love rather than enacting a distinct relational undertaking.

Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Roxane presents the utterance as the conclusion of a reflective progression: she first loved Christian's beauty, then his beauty and soul, and now his soul alone. She sincerely attributes the balcony eloquence and letters to Christian, while the supplied scene establishes that Cyrano supplied the words. Christian therefore interprets her avowal as evidence that she really loves Cyrano. This dramatic misattribution complicates whom her love effectively concerns but does not alter the utterance's core force as an avowal.
