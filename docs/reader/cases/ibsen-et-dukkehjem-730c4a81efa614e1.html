My poor friend. I knew, of course, that I would not be allowed to keep him for long. But so soon—. And then he hides himself away like a wounded animal. NORA. When it has to happen, it is best that it happen without words. Is that not so, Torvald? HELMER (paces up and down). He was so closely bound up with us. I do not think I can imagine him gone. He, with his sufferings and his loneliness, gave, as it were, a clouded background to our sunlit happiness.—Well, perhaps it is best this way. For him, at any rate. (stops.) And perhaps for us too, Nora. Now the two of us are left entirely to each other alone. (puts his arms around her.) Oh, my beloved wife; I do not think I can hold you tightly enough. Do you know, Nora—many a time I wish that some imminent danger might threaten you, so that I could risk life and blood and everything for your sake. NORA (tears herself free and says loudly and resolutely). Now you shall read your letters, Torvald. HELMER. No, no, not tonight. I want to be with you, my beloved wife. NORA. With the thought of your friend’s death—? HELMER. You are right. This has shaken us both; something ugly has come between us; thoughts of death and dissolution. We must seek release from this.
