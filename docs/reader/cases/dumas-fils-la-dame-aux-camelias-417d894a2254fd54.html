How the scores were assigned

“Enfin, je vous aimais, Armand” is a retrospective first-person avowal presenting Marguerite’s love for Armand as a real past emotional state. Although that love motivates her ensuing sacrifice, the sentence itself does not undertake a commitment. Its reflective written formulation supplies no independent evidence of expressive/reflex-like discharge.

Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Marguerite uses the avowal to explain why Monsieur Duval’s appeal moves her to renounce Armand for his and his sister’s future. Her later acknowledgment that the moment’s exaltation may have exaggerated her impressions qualifies her interpretation of the surrounding noble sentiments, but she explicitly insists that this was what she felt. The sacrificial decision demonstrates the importance and disinterested character of her love, without turning this particular sentence into a performative undertaking or reflexive discharge.

What might have happened may still happen. “Are you certain, moreover, that the life you would leave for him would not attract you again? Are you certain, you who have loved him, that you will not love another? Will you not, in the end, suffer from the obstacles your relationship will place in your lover’s life, and for which you may be unable to console him if, with age, thoughts of ambition succeed dreams of love?
