De Guiche is responding to Cyrano’s accusation of revenge. (Cards, dice, smoke subside, fall, fly away with increasing satisfaction): Had I been present when the sash slipped off —Our courage, sir, differs in this respect— I would have picked it up and put it on. DE GUICHE: Yes, more Gascon boasting! CYRANO: Boasting?... Lend it to me. I offer to go up, this very evening, First into the assault, with it across my chest. DE GUICHE: Another Gascon offer! You know that the sash Remained with the enemy, on the banks of the Scarpe, In a place that grapeshot has since riddled— Where no one can go to retrieve it! CYRANO (drawing the white sash from his pocket and holding it out to him): Here it is. (Silence. The cadets stifle their laughter in their cards and in their dice cups. De Guiche turns around and looks at them: they immediately resume their gravity, their games; one of them whistles with indifference the mountain tune played by the fife.) DE GUICHE (taking the sash): Thank you. With this scrap of light-colored cloth, I shall Be able to give a signal—which I was hesitating to give. (He goes to the embankment, climbs it, and waves the sash several times in the air.) ALL: Eh! THE SENTRY (at the top of the embankment): That man down there, running away!... DE GUICHE (coming back down): He is a sham Spanish spy. He renders us Great services. The information he carries To the enemy is what I give him, so That their decisions can be influenced. CYRANO: He is a scoundrel! DE GUICHE (nonchalantly tying on his sash): It is very convenient. What were we saying?... —Ah! I was about to tell you something. This very night, Attempting one supreme stroke to resupply us, The marshal went off toward Dourlens, without drums; The King's sutlers are there; across the plowed fields He will join them; but in order to return without mishap, He took so many troops with him That anyone attacking us would certainly have an easy time of it: Half the army is absent from the camp! CARBON: Yes, if the Spaniards knew, it would be serious. But they do not know of this departure? DE GUICHE: They know. They are going to attack us. CARBON: Ah! DE GUICHE: My sham spy Came to warn me of their assault. He added: “I can determine its location; At what point do you want the attack to take place? I shall say that it is the least defended of all, And the force of the attack will fall upon it.”—I answered: “Very well. Leave the camp. Keep your eyes on the line: It will be at the point from which I signal to you.” CARBON (to the cadets): Gentlemen, prepare yourselves! (They all rise. Sound of swords and sword belts being buckled.) DE GUICHE: It will be in an hour. FIRST CADET: Ah!... very well!... (They all sit down again. The interrupted game resumes.) DE GUICHE (to Carbon): We must gain time. The marshal is returning. CARBON: And to gain time? DE GUICHE: You will be so kind As to have yourselves killed. CYRANO: Ah! So that is the revenge? DE GUICHE: I shall not claim that, if I loved you, I would have chosen you and your men; but, Since no courage compares with yours, It is my King I serve by serving my rancor. CYRANO (saluting): Allow me to be grateful to you, sir. DE GUICHE (saluting): I know that you like to fight one against a hundred. You will not complain of lacking work. (He climbs back up, with Carbon.) CYRANO (to the cadets): Well then! To Gascony's coat of arms, Which bears six chevrons, gentlemen, azure and gold, We are going to add a chevron of blood that it still lacked!
