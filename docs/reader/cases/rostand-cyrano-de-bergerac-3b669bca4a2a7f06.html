How the scores were assigned

Cyrano’s “je vous aime” is foremost a sincere avowal presenting his love for Roxane as true. It also has independently supported expressive/reflexive force: he announces that he will cast out whatever words come to him without arranging them, and the declaration emerges amid suffocation, agitation, and loss of verbal control. No relational undertaking is enacted by the core words themselves.

Ambiguity noted by the annotator

The degree of E is somewhat interpretive: the highly wrought verse remains a lucid avowal, but the explicit framing as an unarranged outpouring supplies more than mere passionate delivery. Cyrano’s concealed identity complicates attribution for Roxane but not the T/P/E force of his own utterance.

Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Cyrano contrasts this speech with his former artificial wit and says that he is finally speaking sincerely from his “true heart.” The declaration expresses his own love, although Roxane takes the hidden speaker to be Christian. Her response—“that is indeed love”—confirms its received force as an avowal, while the subsequent claim that he would sacrifice his happiness for hers elaborates the character of the feeling rather than turning the highlighted words into a commitment.

AI-generated analytical aid, supplied to the annotator; scope: wide context.

CYRANO (growing more and more overcome with emotion): Let us savor for a while... This opportunity that offers itself...to be able To speak softly to each other, without seeing each other. ROXANE: Without seeing each other? CYRANO: Why yes, it is delightful. We can barely make each other out. You see the blackness of a long trailing cloak, I glimpse the whiteness of a summer dress: I am nothing but a shadow, and you nothing but light! You cannot know what these moments are to me! If ever I was eloquent... ROXANE: You were! CYRANO: Until now, my speech has never come From my true heart... ROXANE: Why? CYRANO: Because...until now I spoke through... ROXANE: What? CYRANO: ...the dizziness in which anyone beneath your gaze Trembles!...But tonight, it seems to me... That I am going to speak to you for the first time! ROXANE: It is true that you have a wholly different voice. CYRANO (drawing nearer feverishly): Yes, wholly different, for in the night that shields me I dare at last to be myself, and I dare... (He stops and, distracted): Where was I? I do not know...all this—forgive my agitation— Is so delightful...it is so new to me! ROXANE: So new? CYRANO (overwhelmed, still trying to recover his words): So new...why yes...to be sincere: The fear of being mocked always grips my heart... ROXANE: Mocked for what? CYRANO: Why, for...for a surge of feeling!...Yes, my heart Always clothes itself in my wit, out of modesty: I set out to pluck down the star, and I stop, For fear of ridicule, to pick a little flower! ROXANE: The little flower has its merits. CYRANO: Tonight, let us disdain it! ROXANE: You had never spoken to me like this! CYRANO: Ah! What if, far from quivers, torches, and arrows, We escaped for a while toward things...more fresh! Instead of drinking, drop by drop, from a dainty Thimble of fine gold, the insipid water of the Lignon, What if we tried to see how the soul slakes its thirst By drinking deeply straight from the great river! ROXANE: But wit?... CYRANO: I used it at first to make you stay, But now it would be an insult To this night, these fragrances, this hour, to Nature, To speak like a love letter by Voiture! Let the sky, with a single glance from its stars, Disarm us of all that is artificial in us: I fear so much that amid our exquisite alchemy The truth of feeling may evaporate, That the soul may be emptied by these vain pastimes, And that the finest of the fine may be the final end! ROXANE: But wit?... CYRANO: I hate it in love! It is a crime, When one loves, to prolong this fencing match too much! Besides, the moment inevitably comes —And I pity those for whom that moment never comes!— When we feel that within us a noble love exists Which every pretty word we utter makes sorrowful! ROXANE: Well then! If that moment has come for us both, What words will you say to me? CYRANO: All those, all those, all those That come to me; I shall throw them to you in a mass, Without arranging them in a bouquet: I love you, I am suffocating, I love thee, I am mad, I can bear no more, it is too much; Thy name is in my heart as in a little bell, And since all the time, Roxane, I shiver, All the time the bell trembles, and the name rings out! I remember everything about thee; I have loved it all: I know that last year, one day, on the twelfth of May, To go out in the morning thou changed thy hairstyle! I have so taken thy hair for brightness That, as when one has stared too long at the sun And afterward sees a crimson circle over everything, Upon everything, when I have left the fires with which thou floodest me, My dazzled gaze lays golden patches! ROXANE (in a troubled voice): Yes, that is indeed love... CYRANO: Certainly, this feeling That overwhelms me, terrible and jealous, truly is Love; it has all love’s sorrowful fury! Love—and yet it is not selfish! Ah! For thy happiness I would give my own, Even if thou wert never to know anything of it, If only, sometimes, from afar, I might hear The happiness born of my sacrifice laugh a little! Every glance of thine awakens a new virtue, A new courage in me! Dost thou begin To understand now? Come, dost thou realize? Dost thou feel my soul, a little, rising through this darkness?... Oh! But truly, tonight, it is too beautiful, it is too sweet! I tell you all this; you listen to me—to me, you! It is too much! Even in my least modest hope, I never hoped for so much! Nothing remains for me But to die now! It is because of the words I speak that she trembles among the blue branches! For you tremble like a leaf among the leaves! For thou tremblest! For I have felt, whether thou willest it Or not, the adored trembling of thy hand Descend all along the jasmine branches! (He wildly kisses the end of a hanging branch.) ROXANE: Yes, I tremble, and I weep, and I love thee, and am thine! And thou hast intoxicated me! CYRANO: Then let death come! This intoxication—it is I, I, who knew how to cause it! I ask for only one thing more... CHRISTIAN (beneath the balcony): A kiss! ROXANE (drawing back): What? CYRANO: Oh! ROXANE: You ask for?... CYRANO: Yes...I... (Softly to Christian): Thou art going too fast. CHRISTIAN: Since she is so overcome, I must take advantage of it! CYRANO (to Roxane): Yes, I...I did ask, it is true...but good heavens! I understand that I was far too bold. ROXANE (a little disappointed): You do not insist any more than that? CYRANO: Yes! I insist... Without insisting!...Yes, yes! Your modesty is distressed! Well then! But this kiss...do not grant it to me! CHRISTIAN (to Cyrano, tugging at his cloak): Why? CYRANO: Be quiet, Christian! ROXANE (leaning out): What are you saying so softly? CYRANO: Why, I am scolding myself for having gone too far; I was saying to myself: be quiet, Christian!... (The theorbos begin to play): One moment... Someone is coming! (Roxane closes the window. Cyrano listens to the theorbos, one of which is playing a frolicsome tune and the other a mournful tune): Sad tune? Merry tune?...What, then, is their purpose? Is it a man? A woman?—Ah! It is a Capuchin! (A Capuchin enters, going from house to house with a lantern in his hand, looking at the doors.)
