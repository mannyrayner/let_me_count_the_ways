How the scores were assigned

De Guiche’s written “Je t’aime” is a direct avowal presenting his love or desire for Roxane as true. Although the letter announces that he is staying and intends to visit her, the love clause itself does not undertake a relational commitment. Nothing indicates that the words function as a reflex-like expressive discharge rather than as a deliberate written assertion.

Ambiguity noted by the annotator

Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Roxane identifies the letter as De Guiche’s and regards his attention as persistent harassment. Its continuation says that he has disobeyed her, remained behind, and intends to come see her after interpreting her earlier smile as encouragement. This frames “Je t’aime” as a calculated courtship or pursuit-oriented avowal, not as a commitment. Roxane then fabricates a different public reading of the letter to persuade the Capuchin to marry her to Christian; that deception does not alter the force of De Guiche’s original written words.
