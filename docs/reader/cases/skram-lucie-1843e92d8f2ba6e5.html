The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Knut has affirmed that he would marry Henny despite the supposed lovers in her past and asks whether she now believes in his love. Overcome with emotion, she embraces him, affirms belief in his love, reciprocates it, and says she is his. The utterance therefore combines a sincere-seeming avowal with acceptance of their relationship and prospective marriage. Her later revelation that the lovers were invented as a test complicates the courtship context but does not undermine the ordinary love-related force of this utterance.
