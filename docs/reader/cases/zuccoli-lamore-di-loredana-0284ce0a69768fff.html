Poi ella s'accorse che lievemente, lievemente, con perizia consumata, le toglieva il cappello e il veletto, e di nuovo inginocchiandosi le posava le labbra sulle mani. Riaprì gli occhi, e guardandolo ai suoi piedi, notò la cicatrice lucida e ardente che gli traversava la faccia come un formidabile colpo di scudiscio. --Sono da lei,--disse.--Sono fuggita. Ho abbandonato Filippo. Lo amo ancora, lo amo sempre, non amo che lui; ma sua madre muore, e io devo fuggirlo.... Sono venuta a ricoverarmi da lei.... Senza levarsi in piedi, slacciò la pelliccia e la lasciò cadere intorno, cosicchè parve che il busto snello sbucasse da quel nido candido e morbido maculato di nero. Guardandosi in giro, ella si vide in uno specchio; non aveva più il cappello, non più la pelliccia; era svestita, quasi fosse tornata nella propria casa; e quell'uomo le stava ai piedi, muto, umile, e già padrone di lei.... Lo spettacolo la rivelò a sè medesima. Si drizzò di scatto, esclamando: --No; che cosa faccio? Sono pazza.... Anche Berto s'era alzato, e mettendosele innanzi, le disse prestamente: --Non fugga: è in casa d'un gentiluomo. Si calmi. Ho bisogno di sapere e di parlarle. Se vorrà partire di qui, io non la tratterrò.... Mi dia l'amara soddisfazione di chiederle perdono. Ho scontato il mio errore. Sono stato veramente colpevole, ma sono parso più colpevole ai suoi occhi, perchè lei ha creduto a un capriccio, mentre io l'ho amata e l'amo con profondo sentimento.... Non fugga, la scongiuro.... Loredana tornò a sedere, raccogliendosi intorno la pelliccia bianca. Non appena Berto la vide così, calma e attonita, uscì dal salotto e ritornò precipitosamente, tenendo in mano una bottiglia e nell'altra una coppa. Nè egli nè la giovane avvertirono il ridicolo di quella corsa; Berto spinse innanzi a Loredana una piccola tavola sostenuta da quattro svelti grifi, e versando, posò la coppa vicino alla sua ospite. --Bevete,--disse,--ve ne prego: vi darà forza. Loredana aveva visto che il liquido gorgogliante era sciampagna; ella fece un gesto per rifiutare, e rispose: --No, non è possibile; mi farebbe male. Il Candriani non l'ascoltava; ebbro di gioia, potendola guardare con l'intensità di un desiderio non più rattenuto, la guardava tutta, bramosamente, dalla testa ai piccoli piedi, la cui punta sbucava dal lembo estremo della gonna. --Io vi farò dimenticare Filippo,--egli disse a un tratto.--Voi non l'amerete più; io sono libero e solo, posso dedicarvi intera la mia vita.... Amatelo oggi ancora, non importa; è giusto che lo amiate; dovrò io cancellare la sua imagine dal vostro cuore. E di repente proruppe: --Sono felice; sono felice di vedervi presso di me; vi siete ricordata che vi ho offerta la mia amicizia in un caso estremo, e questo mi consola di molti dolori.... Ah vi assicuro, Loredana, che non sono più lo sventato che avete conosciuto un giorno! Vi amo teneramente e spero di potervi rassicurare.... Si guardò intorno, e vedendo la coppa, la portò alle labbra che sentiva arse da un'interna febbre. --Ascoltatemi,--proseguì imperiosamente.--Sono qui, non perchè vi ami, non perchè io creda alla vostra amicizia, ma perchè voglio e devo perdermi. --Loredana, Loredana, per carità!--interruppe Berto. --Devo perdermi. Loredana lo respinse. --Io voleva uccidermi,--proseguì,--ma sarebbe stato un nuovo scandalo; avrebbero forse accusato Filippo della mia morte.... Ah come sono maligni tutti!... Mi è stato detto in faccia che io sono la vostra amante, che la mia onestà non vale nulla perchè nessuno mi crede, che un uomo è ladro quando tutti lo dicono ladro.... Quante cose ho imparato, spaventevoli! E allora ho pensato che avevano ragione. Il solo che non dubitava di me era Filippo; egli crede al mio amore e alla mia onestà, di cui tutti ridono; e bisogna dunque ch'egli pure non creda più, perchè si salvi.... Ho pensato che poichè mi dicono vostra amante, ogni sforzo è inutile, e io non potrò più liberarmi da questa accusa.... --Non parlare così,--interruppe Berto.--Ti fa troppo male.... Ella lo fissò con gli occhi sbarrati; quel «tu» le parve più brutale d'un bacio che le avesse chiuso improvvisamente la bocca; ma Berto se ne avvide, e soggiunse: --Vi chiedo scusa; non volevo offendervi. Loredana rabbrividì; raccolta la pelliccia, cercò degli occhi il cappello. Ma mentre stava per riprenderlo, si arrestò quasi folgorata da un pensiero. Dove andava? A casa, sua? E Filippo a quell'ora doveva aver già letto le poche righe che Loredana gli aveva lasciato: «Non ti dimenticherò mai; ti amerò sempre; quanto più ti parrò lontana, tanto più sarò tua....» E dopo questo, ella sarebbe tornata da lui, a capo basso, a guisa d'una scolaretta pentita, e sempre troppo tardi per essere perdonata?

Berto saw her turn frightfully pale at once; drawing an armchair near for her, he made her sit down, knelt before her, and Loredana remained like that for a moment, her face white, her eyes closed, while Berto kept kissing her gloved hands. Then she became aware that gently, gently, with consummate skill, he was removing her hat and veil, and, kneeling again, was pressing his lips to her hands. She reopened her eyes, and, looking at him at her feet, noticed the shiny, burning scar that crossed his face like a formidable lash of a riding crop. “I have come to you,” she said. “I have fled. I have left Filippo. I still love him, I love him always, I love no one but him; but his mother is dying, and I must flee from him.... I have come to take refuge with you.... How hot it is in this room; I feel as though I am suffocating!” Without rising to his feet, he unfastened her fur coat and let it fall around her, so that her slender torso seemed to emerge from that white, soft nest mottled with black. Looking around, she saw herself in a mirror; she no longer had her hat, no longer her fur coat; she was undressed, as though she had returned to her own home; and that man was at her feet, mute, humble, and already her master.... The sight revealed her to herself. She sprang upright, exclaiming: “No; what am I doing? I am mad....” Berto too had risen, and, placing himself before her, said quickly: “Do not flee: you are in the home of a gentleman. Calm yourself. I need to know and to speak to you. If you wish to leave here, I shall not detain you.... Grant me the bitter satisfaction of asking your forgiveness. I have paid for my error. I was truly guilty, but I appeared guiltier in your eyes because you believed it a whim, whereas I loved you and love you with profound feeling.... Do not flee, I implore you....” Loredana sat down again, gathering the white fur coat around herself. As soon as Berto saw her thus, calm and astonished, he left the drawing room and returned precipitately, holding a bottle in one hand and a glass in the other. Neither he nor the young woman noticed the ridiculousness of that dash; Berto pushed toward Loredana a small table supported by four slender griffins and, pouring, set the glass beside his guest. “Drink,” he said, “I beg you: it will give you strength.” Loredana had seen that the bubbling liquid was champagne; she made a gesture of refusal and replied: “No, it is not possible; it would make me ill.” Candriani was not listening to her; drunk with joy, able to gaze at her with the intensity of a desire no longer restrained, he looked over all of her avidly, from her head to her small feet, the tips of which emerged from the very edge of her skirt. “I shall make you forget Filippo,” he said suddenly. “You will no longer love him; I am free and alone, I can devote my entire life to you.... Love him still today, it does not matter; it is right that you should love him; it will be for me to erase his image from your heart.” And suddenly he burst out: “I am happy; I am happy to see you beside me; you remembered that I offered you my friendship in a desperate situation, and that consoles me for many sorrows.... Ah, I assure you, Loredana, that I am no longer the thoughtless man you once knew! I love you tenderly, and I hope I can reassure you....” She cut off his words with a gesture. “No,” she replied. “I shall never love you.” She looked around and, seeing the glass, raised it to her lips, which felt parched by an inner fever. “Listen to me,” she continued imperiously. “I am here not because I love you, not because I believe in your friendship, but because I wish and must ruin myself.” “Loredana, Loredana, for pity’s sake!” Berto interrupted. “I must ruin myself. He is about to commit a crime. His sick mother asked him to leave me, and he refused.... No, I cannot accept this sacrifice; it is a horrible thing; I must leave him, and in such a way that he will no longer seek me, no longer desire me.... I do not wish to harm his mother, who has already suffered so much because of me.... If she were to die, ah, if she were to die, what remorse, what shame!... And one day he would awaken from this madness; and between him and me, always, always, I would see his mother’s corpse.... I must prevent him from dishonoring himself!” Berto, standing upright, listened with a sense of wonder to the young woman, who spoke rapidly, agitated by violent excitement; her first pallor had given way to a feverish flush that crimsoned her cheeks, made her eyes shine intensely, and reddened her lips, giving them the color of living blood. She pleased him so much, stretched out in the armchair and sunk into the snowy fur, that Berto bent down once more to kiss her hands. Loredana pushed him away. “I wanted to kill myself,” she continued, “but it would have been a new scandal; they might have accused Filippo of my death.... Ah, how malicious they all are!... I was told to my face that I am your mistress, that my honor is worth nothing because no one believes me, that a man is a thief when everyone calls him a thief.... How many frightful things I have learned! And then I thought they were right. The only one who did not doubt me was Filippo; he believes in my love and in my honor, which everyone laughs at; and so he too must cease to believe, so that he may save himself.... I thought that, since they call me your mistress, every effort is useless, and I shall never again be able to free myself from this accusation....” “Do not speak like that,” Berto interrupted. “It hurts you too much....” She stared at him with wide eyes; that informal “you” seemed to her more brutal than a kiss that had suddenly closed her mouth; but Berto noticed and added: “I beg your pardon; I did not mean to offend you. I love you, and I did not know how to control myself.” “No,” said Loredana, rising, “let me go!” Berto dared to lay a hand on her. “I beg you,” he murmured, “stay a little longer.....” Loredana shuddered; gathering up the fur coat, she looked for her hat. But just as she was about to take it, she stopped as though struck by a thought. Where was she going? To her own home? Filippo would take her back. To Filippo’s home? His mother would die of it. And by then Filippo must already have read the few lines Loredana had left him: “I shall never forget you; I shall always love you; the farther away I seem to you, the more I shall be yours....” And after that, would she return to him, her head bowed, like a repentant schoolgirl, and always too late to be forgiven?
