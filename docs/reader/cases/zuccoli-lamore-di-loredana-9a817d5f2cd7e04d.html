We became heated; he threatened to disinherit me, and I told him I did not give a damn; apparently, Uncle’s two million have thus gone up in smoke, but I prefer my full and absolute freedom to them. This is nothing new: do you remember that I had told you? My relatives do not understand, and arguments are of no use....” “They want you to leave me?” Loredana pressed him. “Of course!” Filippo replied. “Poor Uncle Roberto is merely my mother’s instrument: she makes him act and speak, and he acts and speaks, simply to have some peace.” “They want you to leave me?” Loredana repeated. “And they want nothing else?” “By Jove!” Filippo exclaimed, laughing. “That seems enough to me....” “No, they might want more,” said the girl after a moment’s hesitation. “They might want you to marry.... Because, Flopi, I do not understand.... If you left me, what would you do? Would you live without mistresses and without a wife? Is that what your relatives demand?... Do they not want you to marry? Do they not have some young lady they like and are offering you?” Filippo fell silent, astonished, and wondered how little Lori, whom he still considered scarcely more than a child, had arrived at the truth with the sole aid of logic. To conceal his embarrassment, he rose and said: “No, no: whatever put that into your head?... Let me look at you....” Loredana made a gesture to keep him away and insisted: “Truly, Flopi, have they never spoken to you about marriage? Do they not want you to marry?” “No, indeed; those are fantasies of yours!” Filippo repeated. “Are you prepared to swear it to me?” “I swear it to you....” “Are you prepared to give me your word of honor?” Filippo hesitated for a moment; but by then he could no longer turn back. “I give you my word of honor!” he said. The girl covered her face with her hands and burst into tears. “What!” Filippo exclaimed in surprise. “Now you are crying? Aren’t you happy? Perhaps you do not believe me?” She stood before him with her arms and breast bare; she wore batiste drawers that came down to her knees, gray silk stockings, and low white slippers: she looked like a dainty little Pierrot. “So pretty,” said Filippo, “and so wicked!
