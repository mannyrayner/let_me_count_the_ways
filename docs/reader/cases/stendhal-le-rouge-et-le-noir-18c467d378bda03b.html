The embedded clause “je t’aime” presents Madame de Rênal’s love for Julien as true; her question concerns whether she loves him enough, not whether she loves him at all. Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Madame de Rênal asks Julien whether he finds that she loves him enough amid an adulterous relationship marked by devotion, sacrifice, remorse, and fear of damnation. Julien’s wounded pride requires visible sacrificial proof of her love, so the question seeks reassurance that her love is sufficiently demonstrated. The surrounding passion and the verb “s’écriait-elle” intensify the avowal but do not turn it into an independently reflexive expression or a commitment.

Is it within my power to love you as a brother?” Julien dissolved into tears. “I shall obey you,” he said, falling at her feet, “I shall obey you whatever you command me; that is all that remains for me to do. My mind has been struck blind; I can see no course to take. If I leave you, you tell your husband everything, you ruin yourself and him along with you. Bear in mind that I shall no longer be able to return if you speak.” She promised; he left, but was called back after two days. “It is impossible for me to keep my oath without you. I shall speak to my husband unless you are constantly here to command me with your eyes to remain silent. Every hour of this abominable life seems to me to last a whole day.” At last heaven took pity on this unhappy mother. Little by little, Stanislas was no longer in danger. Remorse remained, and it was what it had to be in so sincere a heart.
