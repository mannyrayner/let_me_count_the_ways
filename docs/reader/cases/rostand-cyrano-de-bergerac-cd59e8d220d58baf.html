How the scores were assigned

Roxane directly avows that she loves the person she believes she is addressing. The coordinated declaration “et suis tienne” (“and I am yours”) also gives the exchange substantial relational-undertaking force. Her trembling, tears, and intoxication establish intense affective delivery but do not independently show that “je t’aime” is a reflex-like verbal discharge rather than a passionate avowal and undertaking.

Ambiguity noted by the annotator

The performative force depends partly on whether “I am yours” is heard as an enacted commitment or as a figurative description of emotional surrender; either reading leaves T dominant.

Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Roxane responds to Cyrano’s impassioned declaration while believing its words and voice belong to Christian. Her love avowal is sincere as presented, but its intended object is bound up with mistaken authorship: she attributes Cyrano’s eloquence and emotional disclosure to Christian. The deception and dramatic irony complicate whom she understands herself to love, without changing the utterance’s core avowal and relational-undertaking force.

CYRANO: Ah! If, far from quivers, torches, and arrows, We fled awhile toward things... fresher! Instead of drinking drop by drop, from a dainty Thimble of fine gold, the insipid water of the Lignon, What if we tried to see how the soul slakes its thirst By drinking deeply straight from the great river! ROXANE: But wit?... CYRANO: I used it at first to make you stay, But now it would be an insult To this night, these perfumes, this hour, Nature, To speak like a love letter by Voiture! —Let heaven, with a single glance from its stars, Disarm us of all our artifice: I fear so greatly that amid our exquisite alchemy The truth of feeling may evaporate, That the soul may empty itself in these vain pastimes, And that the height of refinement may be the end of everything! ROXANE: But wit?... CYRANO: I hate it in love! It is a crime, When one loves, to prolong this fencing match too much! Besides, the moment inevitably comes —And I pity those for whom that moment never comes!— When we feel that a noble love exists within us, Which every pretty word we utter makes sad! ROXANE: Well then! If that moment has come for us both, What words will you say to me? CYRANO: All those, all those, all those That come to me—I shall fling them to you in a bunch, Without arranging them into a bouquet: I love you, I am suffocating, I love you, I am mad, I can bear no more, it is too much; Your name is in my heart as in a little bell, And since I am trembling all the time, Roxane, All the time the bell is shaken, and the name rings out! I remember everything about you; I have loved everything: I know that last year, one day, on the twelfth of May, You changed your hairstyle to go out in the morning! I have so taken your hair for light That, as when one has stared too long at the sun And afterward sees a crimson circle upon everything, Upon everything, when I have left the blaze with which you flood me, My dazzled gaze casts golden patches! ROXANE (in a troubled voice): Yes, that truly is love... CYRANO: Indeed, this feeling That overwhelms me, terrible and jealous, truly is Love; it has all love's sorrowful fury! Love—and yet it is not selfish! Ah! How gladly I would give my happiness for yours, Even if you were never to know of it, If only, now and then, from afar, I might hear The happiness born of my sacrifice laugh a little! —Every glance of yours brings forth a new virtue, A new courage in me! Are you beginning To understand now? Come, do you realize? Do you feel my soul, a little, rising through this darkness?... Oh! But truly, tonight, it is too beautiful, it is too sweet! I am telling you all this, and you are listening to me—to me, you! It is too much! Even in my least modest hope, I never hoped for so much! Nothing remains for me But to die now! It is because of the words I speak that she trembles among the blue branches! For you are trembling like a leaf among the leaves! For you are trembling! For I have felt, whether you wished it Or not, the adored trembling of your hand Descend all along the jasmine branches! (He frantically kisses the tip of a hanging branch.) ROXANE: Yes, I am trembling, and I am weeping, and I love you, and I am yours! And you have intoxicated me! CYRANO: Then let death come! This intoxication—it is I, I, who knew how to cause it! I ask for only one thing more... CHRISTIAN (beneath the balcony): A kiss! ROXANE (drawing back): What? CYRANO: Oh! ROXANE: You are asking for?... CYRANO: Yes... I... (Softly to Christian): You are going too fast. CHRISTIAN: Since she is so deeply moved, I must take advantage of it! CYRANO (to Roxane): Yes, I... I did ask, it is true... but good heavens! I understand that I was far too bold. ROXANE (a little disappointed): You do not insist any more than that? CYRANO: Yes! I insist... Without insisting!... Yes, yes! Your modesty is distressed! Well then! But that kiss... do not grant it to me! CHRISTIAN (to Cyrano, pulling him by his cloak): Why? CYRANO: Be quiet, Christian! ROXANE (leaning forward): What are you saying under your breath? CYRANO: Why, I am scolding myself for having gone too far; I was saying to myself: be quiet, Christian!... (The theorbos begin to play): One moment!... Someone is coming! CHRISTIAN: What does he want?... CYRANO (showing him an uphill street): This way! Straight ahead—always straight ahead... THE CAPUCHIN: For your sake, I shall—thank you— Say my rosary as far as the great bead. (He exits.) CYRANO: Good luck! My good wishes follow your cowl! (He goes back down toward Christian.) Scene 3.IX. Cyrano, Christian. CHRISTIAN: Obtain that kiss for me!... CYRANO: No! CHRISTIAN: Sooner or later!... CYRANO: That is true! It will come, that moment of intoxicated rapture When your mouths will move toward one another, because Of your blond mustache and her rosy lip! (To himself): I would rather it were because of... (The sound of the shutters reopening; Christian hides beneath the balcony.) Scene 3.X. Cyrano, Christian, Roxane. ROXANE (coming forward onto the balcony): Is that you? We were speaking of... of... a... CYRANO: Kiss! The word is sweet. I do not see why your lip dares not speak it; If the word already burns it, what will the deed be like? Do not make it into something terrifying: Did you not just now, almost imperceptibly, Leave jesting behind and glide without alarm From smiles to sighs, and from sighs to tears! Glide a little farther in the same imperceptible way: From tears to a kiss there is only a shiver! ROXANE: Be silent! CYRANO: A kiss—when all is said and done, what is it?
