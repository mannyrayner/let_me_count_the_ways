How the scores were assigned

Jo directly avows her sisterly love for Beth, specifically praising Beth’s effort to overcome her shyness. The utterance presents a loving emotional attitude as true. It encourages and reassures Beth, but does not undertake a commitment, and there is no positive evidence that the words are a reflex-like affective discharge rather than an affectionate avowal.

The avowal is embedded in Jo’s praise of Beth’s decision to attend the outing despite her shyness. “For it” identifies Beth’s effort as the immediate ground of Jo’s affectionate commendation. The speech also functions as encouragement—explicitly compared with the lift supplied by a cheery word—but that interpersonal effect does not by itself create performative or expressive/reflexive force.
