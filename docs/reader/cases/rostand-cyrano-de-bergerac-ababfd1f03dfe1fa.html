How the scores were assigned

Cyrano’s “Je t’aime” is unequivocally a sincere avowal of his loving state (T). It also has independently supported expressive force: he explicitly abandons polished artifice and describes himself as flinging out whatever words come to him, with the utterance emerging amid emotional overwhelm (E). It does not itself undertake a relational commitment, and T plus E adequately represent its core force.

The degree of E is somewhat interpretive because this remains an eloquent dramatic declaration; however, the explicit framing as an unarranged outpouring from the heart supports more than merely passionate delivery. Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

The declaration is sincere as Cyrano’s avowal but occurs within an identity deception: Roxane understands the voice and words as Christian’s. Cyrano stresses that he is finally speaking from his “true heart,” without the wit and artifice that previously screened his feelings. His promise to throw out the words as they come, “without arranging them in a bouquet,” and the surrounding language of suffocation and emotional excess present the words partly as affective overflow. The subsequent elaboration confirms enduring, attentive, self-sacrificing love, while Roxane explicitly recognizes the speech as love. None of this makes the highlighted words a relational undertaking by Cyrano, since he remains hidden and cannot openly establish such a commitment with her.

CYRANO (growing more and more overcome with emotion): Allow us awhile to take advantage... Of this opportunity that offers itself...to be able To speak softly to each other, without seeing each other. ROXANE: Without seeing each other? CYRANO: Why yes, it is delightful. We can scarcely make each other out. You see the blackness of a long trailing cloak, I glimpse the whiteness of a summer dress: I am but a shadow, and you but a radiance! You have no idea what these moments are to me! If sometimes I was eloquent... ROXANE: You were! CYRANO: Until now, my speech has never issued From my true heart... ROXANE: Why? CYRANO: Because...until now I spoke through... ROXANE: What? CYRANO: ...the dizziness in which anyone beneath your gaze Trembles!...But tonight, it seems to me... That I am going to speak to you for the first time! ROXANE: It is true that you have an altogether different voice. CYRANO (drawing nearer feverishly): Yes, altogether different, for in the night that protects me I dare at last to be myself, and I dare... (He stops and, distractedly): Where was I? I do not know...all this—forgive my agitation— Is so delightful...it is so new to me! ROXANE: So new? CYRANO (deeply shaken, still trying to recover his words): So new...why yes...to be sincere: The fear of being mocked always constricts my heart... ROXANE: Mocked for what? CYRANO: Why, for...for an outburst!...Yes, my heart Always clothes itself in my wit, out of modesty: I set out to pluck down the star, and I stop, For fear of ridicule, to gather the little flower! ROXANE: The little flower has its merits. CYRANO: Tonight, let us disdain it! ROXANE: You had never spoken to me like that! CYRANO: Ah! if, far from quivers, torches, and arrows, We could escape awhile toward things...more refreshing! Instead of drinking drop by drop, from a dainty Thimble of fine gold, the insipid water of the Lignon, What if we tried to see how the soul quenches its thirst By drinking deeply straight from the great river! ROXANE: But wit?... CYRANO: I used it at first to make you stay, But now it would be an insult To this night, these perfumes, this hour, Nature, To speak like a love letter by Voiture! Let us allow the sky, with a single glance from its stars, To disarm us of all our artifice: I fear so greatly that amid our exquisite alchemy The truth of feeling may evaporate, That the soul may empty itself in these vain pastimes, And that the height of refinement may be the end of all ends! ROXANE: But wit?... CYRANO: I hate it in love! It is a crime, When one loves, to prolong this fencing match too far! Besides, the moment inevitably comes —And I pity those for whom that moment never comes!— When we feel that within us there exists a noble love That every pretty word we utter makes sorrowful! ROXANE: Well then! If that moment has come for us both, What words will you say to me? CYRANO: All those, all those, all those That come to me, I shall fling them to you in a mass, Without arranging them in a bouquet: I love you, I am suffocating, I love thee, I am mad, I can bear no more, it is too much; Thy name is in my heart as in a little bell, And since all the time, Roxane, I shudder, All the time the bell stirs, and the name rings out! Of thee, I remember everything, I have loved everything: I know that last year, one day, on the twelfth of May, To go out in the morning thou changed thy hairstyle! I have so utterly taken thy hair for radiance That, as when one has stared too long at the sun, One afterward sees a crimson circle upon everything, Upon everything, once I have left the fires with which thou floodest me, My dazzled gaze casts golden patches! ROXANE (in a troubled voice): Yes, that truly is love... CYRANO: Indeed, this feeling That invades me, terrible and jealous, truly is Love; it has all love’s sorrowful fury! Love—and yet it is not selfish! Ah! How gladly I would give my happiness for thine, Even if thou shouldst never know anything of it, If only, sometimes, from afar, I might hear The happiness born of my sacrifice laugh a little! Every glance from thee awakens a new virtue, A new courage in me! Dost thou begin To understand now? Come, dost thou realize? Dost thou feel my soul, a little, rising through this darkness?... Oh! But truly, tonight, it is too beautiful, it is too sweet! I say all this to you, and you listen to me—you, to me! It is too much! Even in my least modest hopes, I never hoped for so much! Nothing remains for me But to die now! It is because of the words I speak that she trembles there among the dark-blue branches! For you tremble, like a leaf among the leaves! For thou tremblest! For I have felt, whether thou wouldst Or not, the adored trembling of thy hand Descend all along the jasmine branches! (He kisses the tip of a hanging branch wildly.) ROXANE: Yes, I tremble, and I weep, and I love thee, and am thine! And thou hast intoxicated me! CYRANO: Then let death come! This intoxication—it was I, I, who knew how to cause it! I ask for only one thing more... CHRISTIAN (beneath the balcony): A kiss! ROXANE (drawing back): What? CYRANO: Oh! ROXANE: You are asking for?... CYRANO: Yes...I... (Softly to Christian): Thou art going too fast. CHRISTIAN: Since she is so deeply moved, I must take advantage of it! CYRANO (to Roxane): Yes, I...I did ask, it is true...but good heavens! I understand that I was far too bold. ROXANE (a little disappointed): You do not press your request any further than that? CYRANO: Yes! I insist... Without insisting!...Yes, yes! Your modesty is distressed! Well then! But that kiss...do not grant it to me! CHRISTIAN (to Cyrano, tugging at his cloak): Why? CYRANO: Be quiet, Christian! ROXANE (leaning out): What are you saying so softly? CYRANO: Why, I am scolding myself for having gone too far; I was saying to myself: be quiet, Christian!... (The theorbos begin to play): One moment... Someone is coming! (Roxane closes the window again. Cyrano listens to the theorbos, one of which plays a frolicsome tune and the other a mournful one): A sad tune? A merry tune?...What then is their purpose? Is it a man? A woman?—Ah! It is a Capuchin!
