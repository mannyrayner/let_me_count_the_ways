I am standing on the platform of the railway carriage. I have stood there all night, wrapped in my cloak. I have watched the evening shadows creep up from the valleys along the mountains, until everything was shrouded in darkness. Now day is drawing near, a pale glow brightens on the horizon; with ever quicker piston strokes, in time with my heart, the train rushes onward toward the foreign city. What strange unrest has kept me awake the whole night? What anxious questions are these for which my heart seeks answers, while I stare broodingly out into the darkness? I cannot, after all, doubt Maria’s love; I no longer doubt my own. Yet I journey trembling to our meeting. For my anxious question is this: Might we not feel like strangers to one another? The Maria whom I am travelling out to find is not the Maria of before. She is not the recklessly enamoured child who in the old days laid herself in my arms, not the cheerful and ever-ready mistress, my conquest and my prize—she is surely both the one and the other, but at the same time something greater and higher, that which perhaps was always the background of her nature, that which I thought I sensed the last times we were together, that which, in the final reckoning, became the Madonna image of my solitary worship. And she—will she recognise me? I scarcely do so myself. I won her in play; I took her sword in hand. Every day the dance we danced was so easy. And now I come to her with unarmed earnestness. Before, it was a matter of a dance; now, it is a matter of life. Before, in rich high spirits, I contented myself with little; now, in poor humility, I ask for Everything. ... We speed onward toward the dawning morning. Suddenly it is black night once more: amid thundering noise the train hurtles through a tunnel. The darkness presses against my chest, there is a rushing in my ears, and, dizzy, I grasp the platform railing. It seems to me that this tunnel will never end; I grope my way forward to reach the lighted carriage—when ... what a revelation! At the purple rim of the sky the disc of the sun rises, spreading its golden halo far and wide, pouring trembling, warm light over moistly gleaming grassy valleys and slopes of grain. Beautiful, fair Day, with joyful gratitude I kneel before You, You who delivered me from my agonising Night and led me, with faith reborn, to Maria. LXI. The golden sun of reunion over mountains, the glittering tears of reunion in silent valleys. In wondering rapture we stand facing one another, unable to find words, unable to ask, unable to answer, able only to sink into one another’s arms and, rejoicing, weeping, in shared bliss praise the rising Day. Was it truly no dream? Was it truly You and me? We look at one another; we shake our heads in disbelief. And yet only now, it seems to us, do we truly know one another. For this is the sublime miracle of our reunion: we meet as so often before we met in the happy dream of our scarcely confessed longing. Now the dream is living and breathing. And You, lovely, living, breathing girl, with the quiet happiness of transfiguration in your roguish eyes, with a woman’s mature thoughtfulness upon your child’s brow, You put your arms around my neck and You say: “So at last it has happened, at the eleventh hour, but not too late—that for which I never ceased to hope. Not something You will regret tomorrow, not something You are imagining now because You have longed a little for your mistress?” I answer: “As surely as I have always preferred to tell You too little rather than too much, so surely do I love You. I love You in joy and in distress, on weekdays and feast days. You are the one with whom I wish to die, but first I wish to live with You for a very, very long time. Indeed, I have longed for my lovely mistress’s caresses. The pews are filled with the country’s most enamoured young women and men. They have flowers in their hands; they sit in pairs, the young women with white roses, the young men with red.
