How the scores were assigned

Lorck explicitly avows that he loves Constance and stresses the seriousness and unexpectedly deep extent of that state, strongly supporting T. His immediate offer—“I give you myself, my life, my soul”—and request that she accept it give the declaration substantial courtship and relational-undertaking force, strongly supporting P. Although he is intensely emotional, the words are delivered as an insistent, developed avowal and offer; there is no positive evidence that their production is itself a reflex-like affective discharge, so E is unsupported.

Narrative situation

The original annotator’s interpretation of the supplied context.

Lorck’s declaration follows an earlier admission that his year-long infatuation has become a consuming passion. After Constance rejects his advances and asks him to stop, he repeats the avowal, calls it serious, offers her his entire self, and asks for acceptance. Thus the utterance combines an avowed emotional state with an attempted relational undertaking. Its force is coercively intensified by his threatening proximity, refusal to accept her resistance, and later accusations and physical restraint; those features characterize the interaction but do not require a category beyond T/P/E. Constance plainly experiences the scene as frightening and insulting rather than as welcome courtship.
