Besides, I have not so long to live that I can make myself unhappy and obey an old man whose very sight makes me grow old. Let him keep his money; I shall do without it.”

“But what will you do?”

Prudence was doubtless about to answer, but I burst in and ran to throw myself at Marguerite’s feet, covering her hands with the tears that joy at being loved so made me shed.

“My life is yours, Marguerite; you no longer need that man—am I not here? Would I ever abandon you, and could I ever repay the happiness you give me? No more constraint, my Marguerite; we love one another! What does the rest matter to us?”

“Oh yes, I love you, my Armand!” she murmured, winding both arms around my neck. “I love you as I had never thought I could love. We shall be happy; we shall live quietly, and I shall bid an eternal farewell to that life of which I am now ashamed. You will never reproach me for the past, will you?”

Tears veiled my voice. I could answer only by pressing Marguerite to my heart.
