Marguerite seemed to be thinking, for she did not answer. My heart beat violently as I awaited her reply.

“No,” she resumed, “I shall not leave Armand, and I shall not hide my life with him. Perhaps it is madness, but I love him! What can you expect? And now he has grown used to loving me without hindrance; he would suffer too much if forced to leave me even for one hour a day. Besides, I have not so long to live that I can make myself unhappy and obey an old man whose very sight makes me grow old. Let him keep his money; I shall do without it.”

“But what will you do?”

“I do not know.”

Prudence was doubtless about to answer, but I burst in and ran to throw myself at Marguerite’s feet, covering her hands with the tears that joy at being loved so made me shed.

“My life is yours, Marguerite; you no longer need that man—am I not here? Would I ever abandon you, and could I ever repay the happiness you give me? No more constraint, my Marguerite; we love one another! What does the rest matter to us?”

“Oh yes, I love you, my Armand!” she murmured, winding both arms around my neck. “I love you as I had never thought I could love. We shall be happy; we shall live quietly, and I shall bid an eternal farewell to that life of which I am now ashamed. You will never reproach me for the past, will you?”

Tears veiled my voice. I could answer only by pressing Marguerite to my heart.

“Come,” she said, turning toward Prudence in an emotional voice, “you will report this scene to the duke, and add that we do not need him.”

From that day onward there was no more question of the duke. Marguerite was no longer the girl I had known. She avoided everything that might recall the life amid which I had met her. No wife or sister ever gave a husband or brother the love and care she gave me. That sickly nature was open to every impression, accessible to every feeling. She had broken with her friends as with her habits, with her former speech as with her former spending. Seeing us leave the house for an outing in a charming little boat I had bought, no one would have believed that this woman in a white dress and broad straw hat, carrying on her arm the simple silk wrap that would protect her from the coolness of the water, was the Marguerite Gautier whose luxury and scandals had caused such a stir four months before.
